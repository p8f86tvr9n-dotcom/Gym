How the supersets work

A1 straight into A2 with no break, then rest 90 seconds, then repeat the pair. Same for B1 into B2. Each muscle gets roughly two and a half minutes off while the opposite one works, so you lose no load and save about ten minutes.

Sunday

Rest. Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Eat as normal — the surplus matters most on the days you are recovering. A walk is fine; anything that leaves you sore is not.

Five rules that outrank the exercises

Rest 2–3 minutes on compounds

Short rests cap the weight you can move, and load is the main driver of growth. Your conditioning is working against you here. 90 seconds is enough on isolation work.

Stop 1–2 reps short of failure

On the final set of an isolation exercise you can go all the way. Everywhere else, leave a rep in the tank so you can repeat the session on Thursday.

Log every set

Weight, reps, how hard it felt. If those numbers aren't climbing month to month, nothing else in this plan matters.

Add reps before you add weight

Given 8–12 reps: stay at the same load until you hit 12 on every set, then add the smallest jump available and drop back to 8.

Keep exercises for 8–10 weeks

You need time to get strong at a movement. Swapping every week feels productive and isn't.

Evening out the left arm

  1. Always start with the left. Do your set, count the reps, then match that number exactly on the right — even if the right could do more. This one rule does most of the work.
  2. Two extra left-only sets, twice a week. Tuesday and Saturday, at the end of the session. One curl, one extension.
  3. Favour dumbbells over barbells on at least two movements a week. A barbell lets the strong side quietly take over.
  4. Check it isn't technique. Film yourself pressing and rowing from the front. Uneven bar path or shoulder height is common, and that's a form fix rather than a size problem.
  5. Measure monthly, not weekly. Cold and flexed, same point on the arm, first of the month. Give it four to six months.

Some asymmetry is normal and never fully disappears. You're closing the gap, not erasing it.

Skinny wrists — what will and won't change

The wrist joint is bone, tendon and skin. There is no muscle there to grow, so its circumference is largely fixed. Bone does thicken slightly under years of heavy loading, but not on a timescale you'll notice.

What you can change is everything above it. The forearm flexors and extensors, and the brachioradialis running from the elbow to the thumb side of the wrist, all respond well to training. Build those and the arm stops tapering — which is the look you're actually after. A thick forearm makes a narrow wrist read as a taper rather than as skinny.

How the forearm work is spread out

  • Monday. Wrist roller.
  • Wednesday. Farmer's walks.
  • Friday. Behind-the-back wrist curls and plate pinches, plus the reverse curls already in that session.
  • Saturday. Hammer levers, towel hangs, wrist curls both directions.

That's four exposures a week. Forearms recover quickly and are used to constant low-level work, so they tolerate more frequency than your chest or biceps.

Three things that matter

Always put grip work last. If your forearms are cooked, your grip fails before your lats do on rows and pulldowns, and you lose back training to save forearm training. Bad trade.

Train both directions. Everyone does wrist curls and nobody does reverse wrist curls. The extensors on the top of the forearm are smaller, but training them adds visible thickness and protects the elbow — most gym elbow pain is an extensor problem.

Go lighter than feels right. Wrist work done heavy turns into arm swinging, and the wrist joint doesn't enjoy being loaded at the end range. Slow, full range, high reps.

Free extras

Stop using straps on anything under 8 reps unless the grip is genuinely the limiting factor. Add fat grips, or wrap a towel round the handle, on your dumbbell rows and hammer curls — it turns work you're already doing into forearm work at no extra time cost.

Eating for actual mass

At 11 stone and 5 ft 11, training six days a week, you're almost certainly eating at maintenance. This is the part that decides whether you get bigger.

  • 3,000–3,200 calories a day to start
  • 150–170g protein a day
  • 0.5–1 lb a week on the scale — weigh 3–4 mornings and average them
  • 7–9 hours sleep, non-negotiable at this training volume

If the weekly average isn't rising after two weeks, add 250 calories a day and check again. If appetite is the problem, blend milk, oats, peanut butter and whey — roughly 700 calories in a glass, drunk in two minutes.

What the next twelve weeks look like

  • Weeks 1–2. It'll feel like you're doing less. The longer rests will feel wrong. Check your logbook instead of your feelings.
  • Weeks 3–6. Strength on the main lifts should be climbing. That's the leading indicator that size is coming.
  • Weeks 8–12. Visible change in the arms and chest, assuming the scale has moved.
  • Every 6–8 weeks. Take a lighter week: same exercises, two-thirds the sets, stop four reps short. You'll come back stronger.
